# War in the North

## General

"The Northern Army had 101,000 men and were supported by 83 tanks, most of them Great War Renaults, 30 BA-6 armored cars and 300 guns of 14 different types, a high proportion of which were obsolete. There were 60 aircraft supported by 40 antiaircraft guns, most of them of light and medium caliber... [The Basque Army] had 37,000 men supported by 16 Trubia M-1936 tanks, ten BA-6, 100 guns (about 20 heavy), and 51 aircraft, including 16 I-15 and Letov S.231 fighters." (E.R. Hooton, Spain in Arms, p86)

## Bilbao

"In [November 1936] ... the Russian [vessel] *A. Andreev* [brought] the Basques **two squadrons of Chatos [I-15 fighter planes], 30 tanks (T-26 and Renaults) ... 40 mortars, 300 machine-guns and 15,000 rifles." (Antony Beevor, The Battle for Spain, p226)

"Bilbao was the port where, on November 1, the Soviet merchant ship *A. Andreu* docked to unload ... **twenty [FAI]** armored cars. (Carlos Fresno, Blindados en España, Part 1: La Guerra Civil 1936-1939, p72)

*NOTE: Antony Beevor claims 14 "Russian armored cars with 37mm cannon" were delivered to Bilbao, but I haven't been able to verify the source he cites, and other sources describe them as FAI armored cars armed with machine guns. The latter seems more likely, considering very few Soviet armored cars used 37mm cannons.*

"The nationalist force was based on ... four Carlist brigades. In addition ... 8,000 Spanish infantry ... supported by **Fiat Ansaldos**." (Antony Beevor, The Battle for Spain, p227)

"The Basques had only a minute fighter force, so the Condor Legion was able to risk using obsolete **Heinkel 51s** as ground-attack aircraft..." (ibid. p228)

"On the northern front ... **three squadrons of Junkers 52 bombers, an 'experimental squadron' of Heinkel 111 medium bombers, three squadrons of Heinkel 51 fighters and half a squadron of Messerschmitt 109s... Savoia Marchetti 81s and 79s as well as their Fiat CR 32 fighters**." (ibid. p228)

"[A] Condor Legion Hs 123 ground-attack aircraft (light dive bomber) was shot down. Meanwhile, the Condor Legion’s 88mm antiaircraft guns were used to subdue resistance from pillboxes." (E.R. Hooton, Spain in Arms, p98)

## Santander

"[Republicans had] only 40 operational fighters and bombers, many of which were obsolete." (ibid. p237)

"On the opening day of the offensive, 14 August, Solchaga's Carlist brigades attacked from the east and smashed through the 54th Division. The Italians were held up by fierce resistance in the Cantabrian mountains ... [but] captured the Escudo pass two days later." (ibid. p237)

"Many of the republican formations then carried out a fighting retreat into the mountains of Asturias." (ibid. p237)

## Asturias

"... approximately half of the republican forces had pulled back into the Asturian mountains, where there was to be a much tougher campaign lasting until the end of October, followed by a further five months of ferocious guerrilla warfare." (ibid. p238)

"The republican air force in the north was down to two flights of **Moscas [I-16s]** and less than a squadron of **Chatos [I-15s]**." (ibid. p301)

# Materiel Availability

"Following the capture of Bilbao, the Nationalists began to employ [the BA-6] more intensively—a trend that intensified after the formation of the Southern Tank Group (*Agrupación de Carros de Combate del Sur*) in November 1938; by the end of that year, the unit’s inventory included **17 BA-6 gun-armed armored cars**." (Carlos Fresno, Blindados en España, Part 1: La Guerra Civil 1936-1939, p78)

*NOTE: Blindados en España, Part 1: La Guerra Civil 1936-1939, p96-97 contains a lot of information about anti-tank guns used by both sides.*

*NOTE: Arms For Spain (Howson, 1999) contains an immense amount of information on Republican war materiel, including an appendix listing every known Soviet arms shipment.*
